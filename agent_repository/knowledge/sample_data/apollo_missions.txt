The Apollo program was a human spaceflight program conducted by NASA, the United States' space agency.
Its primary goal was to land the first humans on the Moon.
Apollo 11 was the first mission to achieve this goal on July 20, 1969.
The mission commander was Neil Armstrong, who became the first person to walk on the Moon.
He was followed by Buzz Aldrin. Michael Collins piloted the command module in lunar orbit.
There were six Apollo missions that landed on the Moon in total, ending with Apollo 17 in 1972.
